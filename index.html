How SLPs are paid

Some districts publish a separate SLP schedule; others apply a set percentage to the teacher schedule or add a stipend.

How to read these numbers

  • Start is step 1 of the column an SLP with a master's lands in, usually BA+45 or MA, plus the SLP stipend. Cypress, Garden Grove and Los Alamitos place every SLP at a higher step, and those minimums are used.
  • Top is the last step with longevity on the highest column reachable without a doctorate, plus the SLP stipend.
  • SLP stipend counts only pay every credentialed SLP gets: Irvine's credential and master's stipends, Santa Ana's stipend tied to Class III step 8, Ocean View's SLP stipend, Tustin's master's stipend and Los Alamitos's ASHA allowance.
  • Max with all stipends adds every stipend tied to something an SLP can hold, such as a doctorate, CCC, state license or National Board certification. Stipends that depend on the assignment (bilingual, coordinator, supervising fellows) are left out. Irvine and Tustin publish this figure as a "Super Max".
  • Per day divides pay by the district's work days, since work years range from 184 to 205 days.
  • Figures come from district salary schedules and union contracts. Every district with a current schedule was checked a second time against its source document.

About this page

This compares pay for school-based speech-language pathologists with a master's degree across Orange County school districts and the County Department of Education. It was compiled in October 2026 from each district's posted certificated salary schedules and union contracts.

It is not an official district or union document. Schedules change when contracts settle, and several districts were still negotiating 2026-27 raises when this was compiled. Confirm current pay, step placement and stipends with the district's human resources office before relying on a figure.

Caseload caps and duty days come from each district's current teachers' or pupil-services contract. Where a contract sets no number, state law applies: Education Code 56363.3 caps a language, speech and hearing specialist's caseload at an average of 55, and 56441.7 at 40 for preschool-only caseloads, unless a waiver is granted. "Not readable yet" means the contract couldn't be read and is still being checked.

Annual figures are for each district's own work year, which ranges from 184 to 205 days. Use "Pay per work day" to compare across different work years.
